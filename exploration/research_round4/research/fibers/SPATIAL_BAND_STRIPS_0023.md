# Spatial strips for one difference band

## 1. Strip decomposition

Fix \(H>0\), and partition the source marks into half-open spatial blocks

\[
A_j=A\cap[jH,(j+1)H).
\]

An active source edge of difference \(h\in[H,2H)\),

\[
e=(x+h,x)\in V(A),
\]

starts in some \(A_j\) and ends in either \(A_{j+1}\) or \(A_{j+2}\).  Define the two strip types

\[
\mathcal E_{j,\varepsilon}(h)
=
\{(x+h,x):x\in A_j,\ x+h\in A_{j+\varepsilon}\},
\qquad \varepsilon\in\{1,2\}.
\tag{1}
\]

Let \(\alpha=(j,\varepsilon)\) range over strip labels, and put

\[
r_\alpha(h)=|\mathcal E_\alpha(h)|.
\]

Then

\[
r_A(h)=\sum_\alpha r_\alpha(h),
\]

and the exact uncolored strip decomposition is

\[
T_H
=
\sum_\alpha\sum_{H\le h<2H}{r_\alpha(h)\choose2}
+
\sum_{\alpha<\beta}\sum_{H\le h<2H}
r_\alpha(h)r_\beta(h).
\tag{2}
\]

Thus no active pair is lost: (2) separates pairs within one strip from interactions between two strips.

## 2. Exact color cancellation in the band

Let

\[
R_\pm(h)
=
|\{(x+h,x)\in V(A):z(x+h,x)=\pm1\}|,
\qquad R(h)=R_+(h)+R_-(h).
\]

Write

\[
S_h={R_+(h)\choose2}+{R_-(h)\choose2},
\qquad
O_h=R_+(h)R_-(h)
\]

for the same-color and opposite-color pairs of active \(h\)-edges.  Direct expansion gives

\[
O_h-S_h
=
\frac{R(h)-(R_+(h)-R_-(h))^2}{2}.
\]

Since \({R(h)\choose2}=S_h+O_h\), one obtains the exact identity

\[
\boxed{\quad
T_H
=
2S_H+\frac{M_H}{2}
-\frac12\sum_{H\le h<2H}\Delta_h^2,
\quad}
\tag{3}
\]

where

\[
S_H=\sum_hS_h,\qquad
M_H=\sum_hR(h),\qquad
\Delta_h=R_+(h)-R_-(h).
\]

Here \(M_H\le M(A)\), because it is the number of physical source cells whose differences lie in the active band.

Identity (3) retains a useful cancellation that disappears if all color pairs are bounded separately.  The desired single-band estimate \(T_H\le(7/5)M\) would follow from

\[
S_H
\le
\frac7{10}M-\frac14M_H
+\frac14\sum_h\Delta_h^2.
\tag{4}
\]

It remains to control only same-colored pairs; strongly imbalanced difference fibers receive the explicit square credit in (4).

The same-color term itself has the exact strip expansion

\[
S_H
=
\sum_{\alpha}\sum_h\sum_{\sigma=\pm1}
{r_{\alpha,\sigma}(h)\choose2}
+
\sum_{\alpha<\beta}\sum_h\sum_{\sigma=\pm1}
r_{\alpha,\sigma}(h)r_{\beta,\sigma}(h),
\tag{5}
\]

where \(r_{\alpha,\sigma}\) counts color-\(\sigma\) cells in the strip.

## 3. Completion clouds for separated strips

Take two active \(h\)-edges of one color \(\sigma\), ordered by their starts:

\[
e=(x+h,x),\qquad f=(y+h,y),\qquad x<y.
\]

Their canonical completion is

\[
q=(y+h,x).
\tag{6}
\]

The three cells \(e,q,f\) form a legal corner of step \(y-x\), so

\[
z(q)=-\sigma.
\tag{7}
\]

When the two strips are spatially separated, (6) crosses the whole interval between them.  It is useful to group such pairs by their actual long completion rather than by the two strip labels.

Fix a source cell \(q=(v,u)\) with

\[
v-u>4H.
\tag{8}
\]

Its possible same-color active completions are indexed by

\[
\mathcal H(q)
=
\left\{
h\in[H,2H):
\begin{array}{l}
u+h,\ v-h\in A,\\
z(u+h,u)=z(v,v-h)=-z(q)
\end{array}
\right\}.
\tag{9}
\]

Every same-colored pair of active edges whose canonical completion satisfies (8) appears exactly once in (9).  Hence, if

\[
S_H^{\rm far}
\]

denotes this far-completion part of \(S_H\), then

\[
S_H^{\rm far}=\sum_{\substack{q\in V(A)\\v-u>4H}}|\mathcal H(q)|.
\tag{10}
\]

This is the exact long-interval completion load generated by far-separated strips.

## 4. Canonical descent below the active band

For a nonempty cloud (9), list its offsets as

\[
h_1<h_2<\cdots<h_k.
\]

For each consecutive pair, put

\[
g_i=h_{i+1}-h_i.
\]

Then

\[
0<g_i<H.
\tag{11}
\]

The four inner marks give two physical source cells

\[
\ell_i=(u+h_{i+1},u+h_i),
\qquad
r_i=(v-h_i,v-h_{i+1}).
\tag{12}
\]

Both have difference \(g_i\).  Condition (8) ensures

\[
u+h_{i+1}<v-h_{i+1},
\]

so the two intervals in (12) are disjoint and ordered.  Thus

\[
\Phi(q;i)=(\ell_i,r_i)
\tag{13}
\]

is a genuine repeated-difference collision below scale \(H\), counted by

\[
\sum_{0<g<H}{r_A(g)\choose2}.
\]

The actual colors \(z(\ell_i),z(r_i)\) are retained in (13).  If they agree, their own long completion is forced to the opposite color; if they differ, (13) is already a bichromatic lower-band pair.  No unavailable translated mark and no unequal-step corner is used.

Let

\[
Q_H^{\rm far}
=
|\{q:v-u>4H,\ \mathcal H(q)\ne\varnothing\}|
\]

and let \(\mu(\xi)\) be the number of ladder links (13) producing one fixed lower-band collision \(\xi\).  From a length-\(k\) cloud there are exactly \(k-1\) links, so (10) becomes

\[
\boxed{\quad
S_H^{\rm far}
=
Q_H^{\rm far}
+\sum_{\xi}\mu(\xi).
\quad}
\tag{14}
\]

The first term is at most the number of long physical source cells, hence at most \(M\).  Every unit beyond the first completion at a long cell descends to an actual lower-band collision.

## 5. The exact many-to-one obstruction

The ladder map \(\Phi\) is not injective.  Fix

\[
\xi=\bigl((b,a),(c,d)\bigr),
\qquad a<b<d<c,\qquad b-a=c-d=g<H.
\tag{15}
\]

A preimage of \(\xi\) has

\[
a=u+h,\quad b=u+h+g,\quad
d=v-h-g,\quad c=v-h
\]

for some \(h\in[H,2H)\).  In particular,

\[
u+v=a+c=b+d.
\tag{16}
\]

Thus all long completions \(q=(v,u)\) mapping to the same \(\xi\) are outer representations of the one fixed sum in (16), nested around the two inner equal-gap intervals (15).  Different choices of \(h\) give different outer representations, and the common-scale restriction places their lower endpoints in an interval of length \(H\); it does not make the representation unique.

This is the precise unsupported interaction.  A symmetric, Golomb-indexed family of outer representations can make \(\mu(\xi)\) large while keeping all \(h\)'s in one band.  The pairwise differences between those outer endpoints are again below \(H\), so large fan-in creates further lower-band collisions, but those secondary collisions can themselves be shared by other ladders.  A proof of (4) needs an amortized bound on this nested reuse.

Opposite-colored pairs of far-strip active edges present a second, simpler limit of completion routing: their corner is already nonmonochromatic, so the long completion receives no forced color.  Their contribution is handled in (3) only through the exact imbalance term; there is no individual completion charge for them.

## 6. Scope

Equations (2), (3), and (5) are the exact spatial-strip and color decompositions.  Equation (14) is the substantive common-scale gain: repeated same-color load at every completion longer than \(4H\) descends canonically to collisions at differences below \(H\).

No \(7/5\) inequality is proved.  The two remaining terms are:

1. multiplicities \(\mu(\xi)\) of nested outer representations over one lower-band collision;
2. near-completion same-color pairs with \(v-u\le4H\), which do not have the uniform inner separation used in (12).

Any global single-band proof using this route must amortize these terms together with the negative square credit in (3).

# Spherical two-distance bands: orthogonal midpoint fibers

## 1. Setting

Let \(X\) be a finite subset of a Euclidean sphere, and suppose that

\[
 \|x-y\|^2\in\{a,2a\}\qquad(x\ne y),
 \tag{1}
\]

where \(a>0\).  Let \(\pi\) be a real linear functional that is
injective on \(X\) and on the finite set of vector differences
\(X-X\).  Thus equality of projected differences is the same as
equality of the underlying vectors.  Replacing \(\pi\) by a positive
multiple does not affect the argument.

For an unoriented nonzero vector direction \(d\), orient it so that
\(\pi(d)>0\), and put

\[
 r_d=|\{x\in X:x+d\in X\}|.
\]

The projected single-band energy is

\[
 T_H(X,\pi)=
 \sum_{\substack{d:\ H\le \pi(d)<2H}}{r_d\choose2}.
 \tag{2}
\]

This is exactly the usual one-dimensional \(T_H\) of the projected
source \(\pi(X)\).

## 2. Repeated vectors are square sides

Suppose that \(d\) has two distinct representations

\[
 (x+d)-x=d=(y+d)-y,
\]

and write \(h=y-x\).  The four points are distinct: an overlap would
give three collinear points whose outer squared distance is four times
one of the allowed squared distances, contradicting (1).

Let \(o\) be the center of the sphere.  Comparing the equalities
\(\|x+d-o\|^2=\|x-o\|^2\) and
\(\|y+d-o\|^2=\|y-o\|^2\) gives

\[
 d\mathbin\cdot h=0.                                      \tag{3}
\]

All of \(d,h,d+h,d-h\) occur as differences of points of \(X\).
If \(\|d\|^2=2a\), then
\(\|d\pm h\|^2\ge3a\), impossible.  By symmetry,
\(\|h\|^2\ne2a\).  Hence

\[
 \|d\|^2=\|h\|^2=a,
 \qquad
 \|d+h\|^2=\|d-h\|^2=2a.                \tag{4}
\]

Thus every collision is a Euclidean square.  The same calculation
also proves that a vector of squared length \(2a\) has only one
representation: two representations would make it a square side and
contradict (4).

## 3. Long pairs with a common midpoint form a cross-polytope

Call a physical pair \(\{x,y\}\subset X\) **long** when
\(\|x-y\|^2=2a\).  Partition the long pairs by their midpoint.  For a
midpoint \(m\), write its fiber as

\[
 \{m-p_1,m+p_1\},\ldots,\{m-p_k,m+p_k\}.
 \tag{5}
\]

Here \(\|p_i\|^2=a/2\).  For \(i\ne j\), the two values

\[
 \|p_i-p_j\|^2=a-2p_i\mathbin\cdot p_j,
 \qquad
 \|p_i+p_j\|^2=a+2p_i\mathbin\cdot p_j
 \tag{6}
\]

are both squared distances between distinct points of \(X\).  They
belong to \(\{a,2a\}\) and sum to \(2a\), so both equal \(a\).  Therefore

\[
 p_i\mathbin\cdot p_j=0.                                 \tag{7}
\]

Every midpoint fiber is consequently a (possibly incomplete in the
ambient space, but complete on its listed vertices) orthogonal
cross-polytope.  Every pair of its axes gives one square.  Conversely,
the two diagonals of every square are long pairs with the same
midpoint.  Hence this is a bijective parametrization of all collisions.

In particular, if \(k_m\) is the number of long pairs at midpoint
\(m\), then the total, unrestricted energy satisfies the exact identity

\[
 T(X)=2\sum_m {k_m\choose2}.                             \tag{8}
\]

The factor two records the two perpendicular side directions of each
square.

## 4. Exact band formula inside one midpoint fiber

Put

\[
 s_i=\pi(p_i).
\]

The square generated by axes \(i,j\) has side directions
\(p_i-p_j\) and \(p_i+p_j\).  Its contribution to (2) is therefore

\[
 w_H(s_i,s_j)=
 {f1}_{[H,2H)}(|s_i-s_j|)
 +{f1}_{[H,2H)}(|s_i+s_j|).                            \tag{9}
\]

Thus

\[
 \boxed{
 T_H(X,\pi)=\sum_m W_H(s^{(m)}_1,\ldots,s^{(m)}_{k_m}),
 \quad
 W_H(s_1,\ldots,s_k)=\sum_{i<j}w_H(s_i,s_j).}
 \tag{10}
\]

Formula (10) retains the actual projection scale; it is not an
uncolored rectangle relaxation.

There is a sharp elementary local estimate.  Form the \(2k\) real
numbers

\[
 Y=\{s_1,-s_1,\ldots,s_k,-s_k\}
\]

and join two when their absolute difference lies in \([H,2H)\).  This
graph is triangle-free: for three ordered real numbers, the two
successive gaps would each be at least \(H\), forcing the outer gap to
be at least \(2H\).  The edges joining different signed axis-pairs are
counted twice by \(W_H\).  If

\[
 P_H=|\{i:H\le2|s_i||<2H\}|,
\]

then Mantel's elementary bound gives

\[
 2W_H+P_H\le k^2,
 qquad
 \boxed{W_H\le\frac{k^2-P_H}{2}.}                       \tag{11}
\]

The same estimate has a dual common-start formulation.  For a fixed
repeated short direction \(d\), the starts of its \(r_d\) translates
are pairwise at squared distance \(a\), by (4).  Their projected
coordinates again have a triangle-free \([H,2H)\)-gap graph.  Swapping
the two sides of each square yields the exact identity

\[
 T_H=\sum_{d:\,r_d\ge2}
 e_H(\{\pi(x):x,x+d\in X\}),                            \tag{12}
\]

and hence

\[
 T_H\le\sum_{d:\,r_d\ge2}\left\lfloor\frac{r_d^2}{4}\right\rfloor.
 \tag{13}
\]

Equations (10)--(13) are the incidence gain supplied by the Euclidean
model.

## 5. A complete \(4/3\) theorem for midpoint rank at most three

Assume now that

\[
 k_m\le3\quad\hbox{for every midpoint }m.                \tag{14}
\]

For \(k=1,2,3\), (11) and integrality give respectively

\[
 W_H\le0,\quad W_H\le2,\quad W_H\le4.
\]

In all three cases, \(W_H\le(4/3)k\).  Since each physical long pair
has exactly one midpoint,

\[
 \sum_m k_m=E_{2a},
\]

where \(E_{2a}\) is the number of pairs at squared distance \(2a\).
Summing the local estimate proves

\[
 \boxed{
 T_H(X,\pi)\le\frac43 E_{2a}
 \le\frac43 { |X|\choose2}
 <\frac75 {|X|\choose2}.}                               \tag{15}
\]

This proves the desired band coefficient for the entire representation
subclass (14), in every band and for every admissible projection.

## 6. The exact high-rank obstruction

The restriction (14) cannot be removed by charging a midpoint fiber
only to its long pairs.  Take

\[
 X_k=\{\pm e_1,\ldots,\pm e_k\}.
\]

Here \(a=2\); non-antipodal pairs have squared distance \(a\), and the
antipodal pairs have squared distance \(2a\).  All \(k\) long pairs
have midpoint zero.  Choose a generic linear functional with

\[
 \pi(e_i)=\frac34H+\varepsilon_i,
 \qquad |\varepsilon_i|\ll H,
\]

and distinct sufficiently small perturbations.  Then every
\(|s_i+s_j|\) lies in \([H,2H)\), while every \(|s_i-s_j|\) is below
\(H\).  Therefore

\[
 W_H={k\choose2},                                       \tag{16}
\]

so \(W_H/k\) is unbounded.  The global ratio remains small,

\[
 \frac{T_H}{{2k\choose2}}
 =\frac{k-1}{2(2k-1)}<\frac14,                          \tag{17}
\]

because the same cross-polytope supplies quadratically many short
physical pairs.  Hence (16) is a limitation of the local long-pair
charge, not a counterexample to the desired global bound.

The overlap that must be controlled is now explicit.  A midpoint fiber
of size \(k\) contains \(2k(k-1)\) short physical pairs, enough to pay
for (11) locally.  But a short physical edge of direction \(d\) belongs
to exactly \(r_d-1\) square completions, one with each other translate
of that direction.  Thus those short-pair budgets overlap with
unbounded multiplicity.  Equivalently, summing the local internal-pair
counts gives

\[
 \sum_m2k_m(k_m-1)=2T(X),                               \tag{18}
\]

rather than a quantity bounded directly by \({|X|\choose2}\).

A useful global consequence of (11) and (8) is

\[
 T_H
 \le\frac12\sum_m k_m^2
 =\frac12T(X)+\frac12E_{2a}.                            \tag{19}
\]

It does not by itself reach \(7/5\): even a separate estimate
\(T(X)\le2\binom{|X|}{2}\) would give only \(3/2\) after using
\(E_{2a}\le\binom{|X|}{2}\).  Formula (15) shows that the loss is
confined to midpoint fibers of orthogonal rank at least four.

The remaining inequality is therefore a compatibility estimate for
the overlapping high-rank cross-polytopes: their signed band graphs
must be amortized against the short physical pairs without charging a
short direction once for each of its \(r_d-1\) completions.  Neither
orthogonality rank nor the independent local inequality (11) supplies
that amortization.  No \(7/5\) bound is claimed for unrestricted
spherical two-distance sets here.

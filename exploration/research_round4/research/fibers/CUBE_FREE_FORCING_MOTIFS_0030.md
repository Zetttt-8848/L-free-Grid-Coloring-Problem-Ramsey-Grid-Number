# Cube-free forcing motifs: a three-link conditional cycle

## 1. A symbolic cube-free source

Fix \(a>0\) and let
\[
A=\{0,a,2a,4a,5a\}.
\tag{1}
\]
Its nonempty positive-difference start sets are
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
d&F_d=A\cap(A-d)\\ \hline
a&\{0,a,4a\}\\
2a&\{0,2a\}\\
3a&\{a,2a\}\\
4a&\{0,a\}\\
5a&\{0\}.
\end{array}
\tag{2}
\]
Every set in the right column is Golomb. In particular,
\(F_a=\{0,a,4a\}\) has the three distinct positive differences
\(a,3a,4a\).

This also proves directly that (1) contains no positive three-generator
additive cube, including cubes with repeated generators. Such a cube with one
generator \(d\) would place a parallelogram
\[
x,x+u,x+v,x+u+v
\]
inside \(F_d\), giving two distinct pairs of gap \(u\) there. That would
contradict the Golomb property in (2).

## 2. The odd three-link motif

On the difference-\(a\) diagonal, put
\[
X_0=z(a,0),
\qquad
X_1=z(2a,a),
\qquad
X_2=z(5a,4a).
\]
The three unordered pairs of starts in \(F_a\) give three corners:
\[
\begin{array}{c|c}
\text{shared diagonal endpoints}&\text{completion}\\ \hline
X_0,X_1&Y_{01}=z(2a,0)\\
X_0,X_2&Y_{02}=z(5a,0)\\
X_1,X_2&Y_{12}=z(5a,a).
\end{array}
\tag{3}
\]
Equivalently, the corner hypergraph contains
\[
\operatorname{NAE}(X_0,Y_{01},X_1),
\quad
\operatorname{NAE}(X_0,Y_{02},X_2),
\quad
\operatorname{NAE}(X_1,Y_{12},X_2).
\tag{4}
\]
These three forcing links form an odd Berge cycle: consecutive links share
\(X_0,X_1,X_2\), respectively. Each link has the exact conditional relation
\[
X_i=X_j=c\quad\Longrightarrow\quad Y_{ij}=-c.
\tag{5}
\]
Thus a short odd cycle of conditional two-color forcing relations is fully
compatible with cube-freeness.

## 3. Why this cycle is not a properness obstruction

The three outputs in (3) are different cells. If
\[
X_0=X_1=X_2=0,
\]
then setting
\[
Y_{01}=Y_{02}=Y_{12}=1
\]
satisfies all three links. There is no odd-parity contradiction: the links
point from the cycle vertices to three separate spokes rather than forcing
\(X_0=-X_1=-X_2=-X_0\).

The remaining corners of the full source (1) are
\[
\begin{aligned}
&\operatorname{NAE}\bigl(z(2a,0),z(4a,0),z(4a,2a)\bigr),\\
&\operatorname{NAE}\bigl(z(4a,a),z(5a,a),z(5a,2a)\bigr),\\
&\operatorname{NAE}\bigl(z(4a,0),z(5a,0),z(5a,a)\bigr).
\end{aligned}
\tag{6}
\]
Together with the assignments above, set
\[
z(4a,0)=z(4a,2a)=z(4a,a)=z(5a,2a)=0.
\tag{7}
\]
Then the six corner patterns, in the order (4) followed by (6), are
\[
(0,1,0),\ (0,1,0),\ (0,1,0),\
(1,0,0),\ (0,1,0),\ (0,1,1).
\]
All are nonmonochromatic. Hence the entire strict source triangle on (1) is
properly two-colored; the three-link odd motif is genuinely nonobstructive.

## 4. The closure step that cube-freeness removes

For a general difference fiber, a pair of starts \(x<y\) gives endpoint
cells \(p_d(x),p_d(y)\) and completion
\[
Q_{x,y}=z(y+d,x).
\]
The completion lies on difference diagonal
\[
d+(y-x).
\tag{8}
\]
If \(F_d\) is Golomb, different pairs of starts have different gaps, so the
corresponding outputs (8) lie on different difference diagonals. This is
exactly what happens in (3): the gaps \(a,3a,4a\) send the three spokes to
the diagonals \(2a,4a,5a\).

The standard four-corner closure needs two forcing outputs to lie on one
common diagonal. That requires two distinct start pairs in \(F_d\) with the
same gap, say
\[
x,x+u,qquad y,y+u.
\]
But then
\[
x,x+u,y,y+u\in F_d,
\]
and translating these four marks by \(d\) produces the positive additive
cube generated by \(u,y-x,d\). Thus the immediate output-pair closure is
equivalent to the local non-Golomb configuration deleted in the cube-free
construction.

Cube-freeness therefore does not forbid short odd Berge cycles of corners;
the example (1) realizes one with three links. It forbids the stronger step
that makes two of their forced outputs parallel and feeds them into a new
corner. Any actual compatibility obstruction in a cube-free source must route
between different output diagonals through additional source coincidences,
rather than closing inside one difference fiber.

Open question attempted (flagged by this run as OPEN): Part D (D-a) by a WINDOW-AVERAGED request budget — find an inequality, valid for every proper ternary source, that bounds how strongly requests can concentrate on few starts, averaged over h (against weights such as a_h, or uniformly over a window), and combine it with double cover to force a proportional loss in sum_e |S_e(h)| = |F_h(D)|.

Facts you may use (prove any you rely on; they are short):
1. Every directed L inside V(D) is (u,d,h), 1 <= d < h: cells (u+d,u) [left], (u+h,u) [corner], (u+h,u+h-d) [up], arm length h-d; it lies in V(D) iff u, u+d, u+h-d, u+h in D; it is monochromatic of color e iff u in S_e(d), u+h-d in S_e(d), u in S_e(h). Hence chi is proper iff S_e(h) ∩ R_e(h) = ∅ for all e,h, and then every start lies in at most two of the R_e(h), so sum_e |R_e(h)| <= 2|F_h(D)|.
2. Exact global identity: sum_{h>=2} N_e(h) = sum_{d>=1} C(|S_e(d)|, 2), since each pair u < u' in S_e(d) is one request at h = d + (u'-u), start u. Hence, by convexity, sum_h sum_e N_e(h) >= sum_d 3 C(|F_d(D)|/3, 2) (state the exact integer version). So the TOTAL request mass is forced by the fiber sizes of D alone, independently of the coloring.
3. Requests at (h, start u, color e) can only sit on starts of the two colors other than chi(u+h,u); so the mass sum_e N_e(h) is carried by at most 2|F_h(D)| (start, color) slots, giving sum_e N_e(h) <= 2 |F_h(D)| * M_h where M_h = max_{u,e} m_{e,h}(u) (trivially M_h <= h-1). In the heuristic extremal regime of the problem, the forced mass over h in a window of length t = 35,000 corresponds to an average multiplicity of about h/97 per occupied slot; so a bound of the form "average over the window of the multiplicity per occupied slot is at most gamma*h + C" with gamma < 1/97 (explicit) would already contradict the regime, and a quantitative version would give the loss delta.
4. The pointwise energy candidate U1 (sum_u m_{e,h}(u)^2 <= (3/2)(N_e(h) + N_e(h)^2/|F_h(D)|) for all sources, all e, h) is FALSE: there is a proper source on D = [0,14) with h = 5, e = 1, |F_5(D)| = 9 where one start carries all N = 4 requests (sum m^2 = 16 > 26/3). Also min_e N_e(h) <= |F_h(D)| is FALSE (D = [0,18), h = 15, |F_h| = 3, N_e = (5,5,5)). Both are small or boundary effects, so whatever you prove must tolerate them (additive error terms, averaging over h, restriction to h far from the span).

Corner/arm language: m_{e,h}(u) = #{d < h : chi(u+d,u) = e = chi(u+h, u+h-d)}: e-agreements between the row word of u and the reflected column word of u+h. High multiplicity at (u, h) means row u and column u+h agree in color e on many arms. Now vary h with u fixed: the column words of u+h for different h are different columns; vary u with u+h = w fixed: different rows against one column. The L's that link two agreements d < d' at the same corner (u+h,u) are: the L with corner (u+d',u) and arms (u+d,u), (u+d', u+d'-d) [arm length d'-d], and the L with corner (u+h, u+h-d') and arms (u+h-(d'-d), u+h-d'), (u+h, u+h-d) [arm length d'-d]. Both corners get forbidden the color e when both agreements have color e. So high multiplicity at one corner forces e to be excluded on a whole family of other cells, which then cannot carry e-agreements or be in S_e. Exploit this: show that a heavy corner (large m_{e,h}(u)) forbids color e on many cells (u+d', u+d'-d) and (u+h-(d'-d), u+h-d') — whose diagonal offsets are d and d'-d... compute them — and that summed over the window this contradicts the forced mass of fact 2 or the requirement that classes S_e(d) be large.

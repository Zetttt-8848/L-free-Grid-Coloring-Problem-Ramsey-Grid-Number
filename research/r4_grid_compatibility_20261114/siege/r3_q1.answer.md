# r3_q1 answer: NTSD via a collision/gadget dichotomy

**First line (status).** I did NOT reach the main target: NTSD(beta, m_0) is not proved here for any beta > 0, and I give no counterexample. What follows are the strongest results I could prove, each labeled, and the exact point where the argument stops.

What is proved:
- (A) A lag-by-lag exact reformulation of TSD properness (Lemma K).
- (B) The whole first-generation subsystem (every L whose spanning cell is an A-pair or a B-pair) is satisfiable exactly when there is no C-collision (Theorem P). The solution has an explicit threshold form. So Proposition O is a special case of a general fact: no argument confined to first-generation L's can prove NTSD for any collision-free instance.
- (C) An explicit mixed-pair count (Lemma M), with constants.
- (D) A class of second-generation conflicts, types I and II, that are checkable from the order of points alone (Proposition 2G). The gadget G1 and its general-TSD form are type II.
- (E) In the translate model, condition (T4) is exactly a split-partition condition on A, which yields a propagation lemma (Proposition SP).

What stays OPEN: the existence of a type I or type II conflict (or any other contradiction) in every near-maximal instance (Section 6).

Conventions are those of the query. (A, B, c) is a TSD instance of span m, U = A ⊔ B, k = |U|, and Q(m) is the Sidon cap. For a lag g, F_g = F_g(U) = {p in U : p+g in U}, and the type of a start p in F_g is the ordered pair (membership of p, membership of p+g) in {AA, AB, BA, BB}. Write α_A(g), α_B(g) for the AA- and BB-starts at lag g when they exist. Each is unique because A and B are Sidon. The short color is γ_g(p) := c(p, p+g).

## 1. Lemma K (lag-clique form of properness) — PROVED

**Lemma K.** A coloring c of the pairs of U, with c = 0 on A-pairs and c = 1 on B-pairs, is proper if and only if the following holds for every g >= 1 and all p < q in F_g. If γ_g(p) = γ_g(q) = f, then c(p, q+g) = 1 − f.

*Proof.* By the definition in C4, the L's of V(U) are exactly the triples of pairs (p,p+g), (q,q+g), (p,q+g) with p < q, g > 0 and p+g, q+g in U; the case q = p+g is included. Given the triple, g is the common length of the two short pairs and p, q are their left ends, so the parametrisation is unique. The triple is not monochromatic iff it is not the case that γ_g(p) = γ_g(q) = c(p,q+g). ∎

Equivalently: at each lag g, the starts of short color 0 are pairwise joined by spanning cells of color 1, and the starts of short color 1 are pairwise joined by spanning cells of color 0.

## 2. Theorem P (the first-generation subsystem is exactly Lemma C) — PROVED

Let Σ_1 be the set of all L's of V(U) whose spanning cell (p, q+g) is an A-pair or a B-pair. In such an L, the mixed cells occur only as short cells.

**Theorem P.** Some assignment of colors to the mixed pairs satisfies every L of Σ_1 if and only if (A, B) has no C-collision, in the sense of Lemma C (C4).

*Proof.* **Decoupling.** A mixed pair (x, y) is a short cell only at the lag g = y − x. So the constraints of Σ_1 split into independent systems Σ_1(g), one for each lag. Σ_1(g) has one variable γ_g(p) for each mixed start p in F_g; the values γ_g(α_A) = 0 and γ_g(α_B) = 1 are fixed.

**The constraints of Σ_1(g).** Take p < q in F_g. The spanning cell (p, q+g) is an A-pair iff p ∈ A and q+g ∈ A, and then the constraint is "not γ_g(p) = γ_g(q) = 0". It is a B-pair iff p ∈ B and q+g ∈ B, and then the constraint is "not γ_g(p) = γ_g(q) = 1". Going through the types:

1. AB-start p < α_A: γ(p) = 1. AB-start p > α_B: γ(p) = 0.
2. BA-start q > α_A: γ(q) = 1. BA-start q < α_B: γ(q) = 0.
3. AB-start p < BA-start q: not both 0. BA-start q < AB-start p: not both 1.
4. AB < AB and BA < BA give no constraint, because the spanning cell is mixed.
5. The pair α_A, α_B gives no constraint: its spanning cell is mixed, or the short colors differ.

Items 1 and 2 are Lemma C1.

**Only if.** A C-collision forces one variable to both values. This is Lemma C.

**If.** Pick a real number τ that is not in F_g, and set:
- for an AB-start p: γ(p) = 1 if p < τ, and 0 if p > τ;
- for a BA-start q: γ(q) = 0 if q < τ, and 1 if q > τ.

Item 3 then holds whatever τ is. If AB p < BA q were both 0, we would have p > τ > q. If BA q < AB p were both 1, we would have q > τ > p. Both contradict the assumed order.

Items 1 and 2 hold for a suitable τ. We need four implications:
- AB p < α_A ⇒ p < τ;
- AB p > α_B ⇒ p > τ;
- BA q > α_A ⇒ q > τ;
- BA q < α_B ⇒ q < τ.

Note that α_A and α_B are not mixed starts, because a point is the start of at most one pair of a given lag.

- *Both defined, α_B < α_A.* No collision means there is no AB-start in (α_B, α_A). Take τ in (α_B, α_A) \ F_g.
  - AB p < α_A forces p < α_B < τ.
  - AB p > α_B forces p > α_A > τ.
  - BA q > α_A > τ.
  - BA q < α_B < τ.
- *Both defined, α_A < α_B.* No collision means there is no BA-start in (α_A, α_B). Take τ in (α_A, α_B) \ F_g. The four checks are the same.
- *Only α_A defined.* Take τ = α_A; the two remaining implications hold. The case where only α_B is defined is symmetric.
- *Neither defined.* Any τ works.

Since the Σ_1(g) are independent, Σ_1 is satisfiable. ∎

**Corollary P1 (PROVED).** Let (A, B) be any collision-free pair, of any size. Then every contradiction in its TSD system involves at least one L whose spanning cell is mixed. So a proof of NTSD must show, for each near-maximal pair, either a C-collision or an unsatisfiable configuration that contains such an L. This strengthens Proposition O from translates to every collision-free pair, and it makes the dichotomy of the query a necessity rather than a choice.

**Corollary P2 (class picture; PROVED).** Suppose there is no collision at lag g. Then in every TSD coloring:
- The starts forced into class 0 are α_A, the BA-starts below α_B, and the AB-starts above α_B.
- The starts forced into class 1 are α_B, the AB-starts below α_A, and the BA-starts above α_A.
- The only starts left free by Σ_1 are the mixed starts strictly between α_A and α_B of the type that is not excluded by Lemma C. When α_B < α_A these are the BA-starts; when α_A < α_B they are the AB-starts.

This is Lemma C1 combined with the no-collision condition.

## 3. Lemma M (mixed-pair count) — PROVED

Let x(g) be the number of mixed pairs of difference g, let ρ(m) := Q(m)^2/m, and let 2 <= T <= m.

**Lemma M.** Assume |A|, |B| >= (1−β) Q(m) with 0 <= β <= 1/2. Then

  x̄_T := 2 Σ_{g=1}^{T−1} (T−g) x(g) / (T(T−1))  >=  4(1−2β) ρ(m) · m/(m+T) − 2(sqrt(2m)+1)/(T−1) − 2.

Also Σ_{g>=1} x(g) = |A||B| >= (1−2β) ρ(m) m.

*Proof.* Apply Lemma 4.2 (C5) to X = U, with s = k, λ = T−1, Λ = T and N = m+T:

  kT + 2 Σ_{g<T} (T−g) r_U(g) >= k^2 T^2/(m+T).

Every pair of U is an A-pair, a B-pair or a mixed pair, so r_U(g) = r_A(g) + r_B(g) + x(g). Since A and B are Sidon, r_A(g), r_B(g) <= 1, so 2 Σ_{g<T} (T−g)(r_A + r_B) <= 2T(T−1). Divide by T(T−1) and use T/(T−1) >= 1. Then insert:
- k >= 2(1−β) Q(m), so k^2 >= 4(1−2β) Q(m)^2;
- k <= 2Q(m) <= 2(sqrt(2m)+1), by (Q-up).

The second claim is immediate. ∎

**Status of ρ.** Lemma 4.1 proves only ρ(m) > 1/8. The statement ρ(m) → 1 is CITED: Singer's construction gives Q(p^2+p) >= p+1 for primes p, and Q(m) <= sqrt(m) + O(m^{1/4}) is the Erdős–Turán–Lindström bound; the step from primes to all m uses density of primes in short intervals (I cite from memory that p_{n+1} − p_n = o(p_n) suffices). With ρ ≈ 1, T = m^{3/4} and m large, Lemma M gives x̄_T >= 2 − 8β − o(1). This is the "two mixed pairs per small difference" of the query. By Lemma S (C6), all but a fraction O(β + T/m + sqrt(m)/T) of the weight lies on differences g in Δ(A) ∩ Δ(B), where the classes of Corollary P2 are defined.

## 4. Proposition 2G (second-generation conflicts, types I and II) — PROVED

Define the **first-generation forced class** φ_g(p) ∈ {0, 1, ∗} of a start p in F_g:
- φ_g(α_A) = 0 and φ_g(α_B) = 1;
- for an AB-start p, φ_g(p) = 1 if α_A(g) > p, and 0 if α_B(g) < p;
- for a BA-start q, φ_g(q) = 0 if α_B(g) > q, and 1 if α_A(g) < q;
- φ_g = ∗ otherwise.

If two of the rules assign different values to one start, that start is a C-collision. By Lemma C1, every TSD coloring has γ_g(p) = φ_g(p) whenever φ_g(p) ≠ ∗.

**Second-generation forcing.** Let p < q in F_g with φ_g(p) = φ_g(q) = f ∈ {0,1}. By Lemma K, every TSD coloring has c(p, q+g) = 1 − f. When the cell (p, q+g) is an A-pair or a B-pair, this is one of the first-generation constraints of Theorem P. When the cell is mixed, call it a **forcing of the mixed cell (p, q+g) to 1 − f from lag g**. For a fixed cell (x, y) and lag g the decomposition is unique (p = x, q = y − g), so a cell can be forced from several lags, at most once from each.

**Proposition 2G.** Each of the following is an order-and-membership configuration in A ∪ B. Each one excludes every TSD coloring.
- **Type I.** A mixed cell (x, y) is forced to 1 − f from some lag g < y − x, and φ_{y−x}(x) = f.
- **Type II.** A mixed cell (x, y) is forced to 0 from one lag g and to 1 from another lag g'.

*Proof.* In type I, the forcing gives c(x,y) = 1 − f, while Lemma C1 at lag y − x gives γ_{y−x}(x) = c(x,y) = f. In type II, the two forcings contradict each other directly. ∎

**G1 is type II (PROVED; check).** Use the general-TSD form of C7.

At lag g = y − p:
- the starts are x (AB, because q = x+g ∈ B), α_A(g) = p and α_B(g) = p + t;
- x < p gives φ_g(x) = 1, and φ_g(p+t) = 1;
- x < p+t, so the cell (x, y+t) is forced to 0.

At lag g' = z1 − x:
- the starts are α_A(g') = x, α_B(g') = x + t, and z2 (AB, because z2 + g' = y + t ∈ B);
- x + t < z2 gives φ_{g'}(z2) = 0, and φ_{g'}(x) = 0;
- x < z2, so the cell (x, y+t) is forced to 1.

This is exactly the C7 proof. The three mirror gadgets are type II by the same check.

**Counting heuristic for types I and II (HEURISTIC, not used).** By Lemma M, a typical small lag carries about four starts. About two of them have a forced class, so there are O(1) same-class pairs and O(1) forced mixed cells per lag, about Θ(m) in total. If the forced class at lag y − x of the left end were an independent fair bit, a positive fraction of these forcings would be type I conflicts. Proposition O shows that order-correlated pairs break this independence at the first generation, and the C9 evidence (two non-colorable instances containing no G1) shows that type II with G1's specific shape does not cover everything. I have not checked whether those two C9 instances contain a type I conflict or a type II conflict of another shape; I could not compute this here.

## 5. Translate model: (T4) is a split-partition condition — PROVED

Use the setting of Theorem T (C7): B = A + t, A is Sidon with no difference in [1, 2t], and the variables are φ, X and Y. For an A-pair a1 < a2 with δ = a2 − a1, write X(a1, a2) := X_δ.

**Lemma SP0.** (T4) holds iff A = P0 ⊔ P1 with X(a, a') = 1 for all a < a' in P0 and X(a, a') = 0 for all a < a' in P1. (Take P_i = φ^{-1}(i).)

*Proof.* (T4) says that φ(a), φ(a') and X(a, a') are not all equal. For a, a' both in P0 this says X ≠ 0, for a, a' both in P1 it says X ≠ 1, and for a pair split between P0 and P1 it says nothing. ∎

Hence the graph on A whose edges are the pairs with X = 0 must be a split graph: P1 is a clique and P0 is an independent set. By the classical theorem of Földes and Hammer (CITED from memory), a graph is split iff it has no induced 2K_2, C_4 or C_5. Every forced label in (T1) is an order bit of A, so an induced 2K_2, C_4 or C_5 among the forced labels is an unsatisfiable configuration.

**Proposition SP (propagation; PROVED).** Let a < a' have difference δ ∈ T, meaning δ, δ+t ∈ Δ, and write b := α_{δ+t}.
- (a) If a, a' ∈ P0, then b > a and b + δ + t ∈ P1.
- (b) If a, a' ∈ P1, then b < a and b ∈ P0.

*Proof.*
- (a) By Lemma SP0, X_δ = 1, so (T1) gives σ(δ) = 1, that is b > a. Then (T1) gives X_{D(δ)} = 0, where D(δ) is the A-pair (a, b + δ + t). Since a ∈ P0, Lemma SP0 gives b + δ + t ∉ P0.
- (b) X_δ = 0 gives σ(δ) = 0, that is b < a; the starts are distinct. Then X_{D(δ)} = 1, where D(δ) is the A-pair (b, a'). Since a' ∈ P1, Lemma SP0 gives b ∉ P1. ∎

Because A is Sidon, the maps δ ↦ (b, b + δ + t) are injective. So the internal pairs of P0 with difference in T inject into A-pairs whose right end is in P1, and the internal pairs of P1 with difference in T inject into A-pairs whose left end is in P0. These counts alone give no contradiction: a pair of size Θ(k^2) against a pool of size Θ(k^2). Turning SP into a contradiction for near-maximal A needs the order statistics of the starts α_δ against α_{δ+t}. This is the same missing ingredient as in Section 6.

## 6. Where the argument stops (OPEN, precise)

**Open Lemma 2G-count.** There exist β > 0 and m_0 with the following property. Every pair of disjoint Sidon sets A, B in an interval of m+1 integers, with m >= m_0, |A|, |B| >= (1−β) Q(m), and no C-collision, contains a configuration that excludes every TSD coloring. Natural candidates are type I or type II of Proposition 2G, or an induced 2K_2, C_4 or C_5 of forced labels in the lag-clique graphs of Lemma K.

Together with Lemma C, Open Lemma 2G-count would imply NTSD(β, m_0). By Theorem P, Open Lemma 2G-count cannot be avoided: every collision-free near-maximal instance must be refuted, if it is refuted at all, through an L with a mixed spanning cell.

**The obstacle.** A type I or type II conflict is a condition on the relative order of:
- α_A(g) and α_B(g);
- the mixed starts at lag g;
- α_A(D) and α_B(D) at the derived lag D = q + g − p.

These are 3- and 4-point order correlations between A and B, and between A and its translates. Lemma 4.2, Lemma S, energy and Fourier-magnitude bounds control only pair counts, and Proposition O shows that pair counts are blind to the order. I found no argument that bounds these order correlations for all near-maximal Sidon pairs. Bose and Singer sets are structured, so random-model reasoning is not admissible. The C9 evidence (no colorable near-maximal pair in the sample) is evidence only for the instances it lists.

**Partial statement that is proved.**

- **NTSD under hypothesis H.** Let H be the hypothesis that the instance has a C-collision, a type I conflict or a type II conflict. Then the instance has no TSD coloring. Each of these is a finite list of membership and order conditions, so H is checkable instance by instance.
- **Consequence for the consumer.** If H holds for every TSD instance of span at least m_0 with |A|, |B| >= (1−β) Q(m), then NTSD(β, m_0) holds. Through the C8 sketch, which is the subject of a separate query and is not proved here, NTSD(β, m_0) would yield FD(η, S_0) with η of order β/200.
- **No R_4 claim.** No bound on R_4 is claimed.
